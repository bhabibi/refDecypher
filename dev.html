126710 BLNR — GMT-Master II “Batgirl.” The five segments join straight back into the full reference. Expand any segment below for every code.

The leading digit marks the generation. The first two digits together also hint at the production era — case proportions and movement family.

Era hint from the first two digits: 11 = 2000s–2010s · 12 = 2020s (current) · 14 = 1980s–90s · 16 = 1990s–2000s · 22 = current precious-metal.

The three digits after the generation identify the family — dive, GMT, dress, chronograph. The manufacturer reuses these across generations and related lines, so the exact model is confirmed by the full reference.

A single digit tells you what's around the dial — and often what kind of watch you're looking at.

Exception: Yacht-Master II references carry an 8 here for the Ring Command regatta bezel — a line-specific identifier, not a general code.

The final digit is the material code — steel, two-tone Rolesor, solid gold, platinum, Rolesium, or titanium. (Bracelet style, like Oyster vs Jubilee, isn't encoded in the reference.)

Letters following the digits describe the bezel insert — ceramic colour combinations, abbreviated in French, sometimes with collector nicknames attached.

The caliber — the engine inside — is not encoded in the reference number. It's a property of the specific model, looked up separately. Each era has its defining movement.

Vintage and recent calibers link to full service manuals; current calibers are listed with their specs.
